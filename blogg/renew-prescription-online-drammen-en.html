How to renew a prescription online in Norway – without visiting the clinic

10 August 2026 6 min read Digital everyday health

If you have recently moved to Drammen, the Norwegian health system can feel like a black box – especially the paperless bits. Here is one that quietly saves you a trip: when you run low on a regular medicine, you can usually ask your GP (fastlege) to renew the prescription online through Helsenorge, using BankID. No phone queue, no time off work. Here is how it works, what is free, and when you should still talk to a doctor.

First, what is an "e-resept"?

In Norway, a prescription is called a resept, and today it is almost always digital – an e-resept. When your doctor issues one, it is sent electronically to a national database called Reseptformidleren ("the Prescription Broker"). It sits there securely, and any pharmacy (apotek) you choose retrieves it when you come to collect your medicine. You are free to pick up at any pharmacy – in Drammen city centre, at Bragernes, or wherever suits you. The system is well established: the vast majority of prescriptions in Norway are now electronic, with roughly 27 million e-prescriptions sent to Reseptformidleren each year.

Because the prescription is already digital, you do not need a paper slip to collect medicine – and the renewal itself can often happen without setting foot in the clinic.

If you are new to Norway

Everyone registered as a resident is entitled to a fastlege – a regular GP who is your first point of contact for non-emergency health issues. Most digital health services, including prescription renewal, require BankID (or a Buypass smartcard) to log in. Setting up BankID through your bank is the single most useful step for accessing Norwegian public services.

Renewing a prescription on Helsenorge, step by step

The simplest route is through your GP's digital services on Helsenorge.no. "Forny resept" (renew prescription) is one of several services under what is called digital dialogue with your GP. Here is the flow:

  1. Log in to Helsenorge.no with BankID or Buypass. Log-in is required because the service handles your health data.
  2. Select your GP and find the "Forny resept" service (available if your clinic has enabled it).
  3. Choose the regular medicine or medical equipment you need renewed, and add a short note if asked.
  4. Send the request. Your doctor reviews it and, if appropriate, renews the prescription – which then waits for you in Reseptformidleren.
  5. Collect the medicine at the pharmacy of your choice. No paperwork needed.

If logging in and navigating Helsenorge is new to you, we have a beginner's guide: How to use Helsenorge.no.

Simple renewal vs. e-consultation – the key difference

This is worth understanding, because it decides both how long it takes and whether it costs anything. A simple renewal is a straightforward request for more of a medicine you already take regularly, where the doctor just confirms you can continue. It usually carries no patient fee.

An e-consultation, by contrast, is written or video contact where the doctor makes an actual medical assessment – for example if a dose should change, you have new symptoms, or it has been a while since your last check-up. That triggers the normal patient fee (egenandel), the same as an in-person appointment, and a reply typically arrives within a few working days. To learn more about when online contact is a good fit, read Video consultation with your GP – when does it fit?

In short: topping up something stable is often free and fast; asking the doctor to judge something medical is a consultation, with the fee that implies.

What can be renewed online – and what needs a visit?

Most regular medicines and much medical equipment can be renewed digitally. But there are important exceptions, and your doctor always decides what is safe:

  • Habit-forming medicines and certain other preparations often require an appointment, for patient-safety reasons.
  • If you have not been in for a check-up in a long time, the doctor may ask you to come in first – for example with blood-pressure or thyroid medicines that need regular monitoring.
  • If a medicine is new to you, or you have had side effects, that belongs in a consultation, not a simple renewal.

Being told something cannot be renewed online is not an obstacle – it is your doctor looking after you. In that case, you book an appointment instead.

Is it safe?

Yes. The whole system runs on national health infrastructure operated by Norsk helsenett (the Norwegian Health Network), and every log-in uses BankID or Buypass. That means only you – and health personnel with a legitimate need – can access your prescriptions. Information is encrypted, and you consent to what is shared.

One thing to remember: a digital renewal does not replace clinical judgement. If you feel different, have new symptoms, or are in doubt, a conversation with your GP is always more appropriate than simply asking for a refill. For acute, serious illness outside opening hours, call the out-of-hours medical service (legevakt) on 116 117; for life-threatening emergencies, call 113.
