BankID and health services in Norway: logging in safely and avoiding scams

19 October 2026 6 min read Digital everyday life

If you are new to Norway, BankID may be the first thing that surprises you: one electronic ID that opens your bank, public services and, importantly, your health services. That makes it convenient, and it is also why scammers are interested in it. Here is a calm walk-through of how logging in works, how to use it safely, and what to do if something looks off.

What BankID is, and why health services require it

BankID is a Norwegian electronic ID, normally issued through your bank. In health services it works much like a digital passport. Health information is among the most personal information about you, so services that show it require the highest level of security when you log in. Helsenorge, the national health portal, explains that MinID (the login many people know from other public services) does not offer a high enough security level. Adults therefore cannot use MinID to log in to Helsenorge.

What you can use is BankID (including BankID on mobile), Buypass ID or Commfides e-ID. For most people, BankID is simplest because it is already used for online banking.

Good to know if you are new to Norway

BankID and Helsenorge are tied to a Norwegian national identity number or a D-number. If you have recently moved to Drammen and do not yet have one, our reception can tell you how to book an appointment while you wait. Helsenorge also has an English-language version of many of its pages.

Three levels of access after you log in

After logging in, you choose how much you want to be able to access. This is called the consent level, and you decide:

  • Basic: you can view health and care information registered about you and ask for some of it to be corrected or erased.
  • Basic+: as Basic, plus hospital medical records where they are digitally available.
  • Full access: all Helsenorge services, including appointments, test results, letters and forms.

To book a GP appointment or renew a prescription online, you need full access. If Helsenorge is new to you, our beginner’s guide covers the basics.

The Helsenorge app: app code and fingerprint

Pulling out BankID every time can feel tedious, so the Helsenorge app offers a simpler routine. The first time you log in, you do so via ID-porten, the government login service. You must be at least 16 and have a Norwegian national identity number or D-number. After that you create a four-digit app code, which you can also link to face recognition or fingerprint. Biometric login has to be switched on again after six months.

According to Helsenorge, the app is as secure as using helsenorge.no. If you lose your phone, you can log in at helsenorge.no and log out to cut off access from that phone.

How to recognise a scam

Scammers often pretend to be Helsenorge, your GP or the hospital. Helsenorge is clear about a few points that make it easier to stop such attempts:

  • Helsenorge does not send login links by text message or email, and its messages do not contain a “Logg inn” (log in) link.
  • Helsenorge never asks for card details, security codes or account numbers by text or email.
  • Be wary of messages that create urgency, claim your account is locked, or ask you to open an attachment.
  • Check whether the sender is unknown, the sender name and email address do not match, or the text reads oddly.

The safest habit is never to log in through a link in a message. Open the app, or type helsenorge.no yourself. Once you are logged in, links in your Helsenorge inbox are safe to use.

If you have already clicked or typed something in

If you entered your BankID or other login details on a fake page, you should contact your bank immediately. You can forward suspicious emails to spam@helsenorge.no so that Helsenorge can detect new scams. You will not get a reply, but you help others. Then delete the message.

Simple habits that make a difference

Helsenorge’s own advice is to password-protect your device, log out when you are finished, keep your browser up to date, and set your browser to delete cookies when you close it. This matters most on shared or public computers. BankID codes and passwords are personal and are not shared, not even with family or friends. If someone needs to help you with health services, the right tool is a power of attorney on Helsenorge.

If others could reach your login, for example your BankID, you can block your access in Helsenorge settings or by using a PDF form. Your health data then becomes inaccessible, and you will not receive digital messages such as appointment letters until you reopen access. Helsenorge’s helpline is 23 32 70 00, weekdays 08:00–15:30.

If you would rather not use BankID

Not everyone wants to be digital, and that is fine. Helsenorge describes options such as giving someone else power of attorney, setting yourself as inactive so you receive important letters by post, or withdrawing consent entirely. Withdrawing consent means you can no longer communicate electronically with your GP or hospital through Helsenorge, and your data and any power of attorney are deleted. A conversation with your GP about what suits you is a good starting point.

At our surgery in Rosenkrantzgata 75 in central Drammen, you can always call or drop by. If you prefer to see a doctor from home, we also offer video consultations, which require secure login.

This is general information, not an assessment of your situation. If you have questions about your health, contact your GP. In a life-threatening emergency, call 113. Outside opening hours, the out-of-hours medical service (legevakt) is on 116 117.
