Your medical record in Norway: how to read it, who has seen it – and how to fix mistakes

23 November 2026 6 min read Digital health

If you have moved to Drammen from another country, Norway's approach to medical records may surprise you: you have a legal right to read almost everything your doctors write about you, free of charge, and to see who has opened your file. Here is how it works in practice – and why your GP's notes are not (yet) where most people expect to find them.

Your legal right to read your record

In Norway, every doctor, hospital and clinic keeps a journal – the Norwegian word for your medical record. Under the Patients' and Users' Rights Act, section 5-1, you have the right to access your record, including attachments. The Norwegian Directorate of Health explains that you can also ask for a copy, and that access should be free – you cannot be charged for copying or postage. Only extra copies beyond the first may carry a reasonable fee.

Some useful points if you are used to a different system:

  • You do not need to give a reason for wanting to see your own record.
  • You can ask for medical terms to be explained. If your record is in Norwegian and you find it hard to follow, it is reasonable to ask your doctor to go through it with you.
  • Exceptions are very narrow. Access can only be limited if it is strictly necessary to prevent danger to life or serious harm to health, and the reason must be written in the record.

What you can see on Helsenorge

Helsenorge.no is Norway's national health portal, and you log in with BankID. Its patient records service shows documents from selected hospitals, including hospitals in the South-Eastern health region that Drammen Hospital belongs to. Typical documents are discharge summaries (epikriser) and outpatient notes.

  • You must be 16 or older to log in yourself. Parents lose access to a child's record when the child turns 16.
  • You can read and download documents, but not add anything.
  • Helsenorge notes that it can take some time before new documents appear.

New to the portal? Start with our guide How to use Helsenorge.no.

What about your GP's notes?

According to Helsenorge, records from your GP (fastlege) are not currently available there. To read what your GP has written, you simply ask the GP office for access or a copy. You have exactly the same legal right as at the hospital.

Asking your GP for your record

Contact the GP office and say that you would like access to your record. It helps to be clear about:

  • What you need – the whole record, or notes from a particular period. This is useful if you need your history sent to a doctor abroad or for an insurance claim.
  • How you want it – digitally through a secure channel, or on paper. Health information is never sent by ordinary email in Norway.
  • Whether you want help reading it – a short appointment with your GP can be more useful than a stack of pages.

The office will always check your identity before releasing anything.

Who has looked at your record?

Every access to an electronic record in Norway is logged. On Helsenorge you can view the access log for the hospital documents after logging in. Normen, the health sector's information security code, states that you are entitled to know who accessed your information, when, and what it concerned. Such requests should normally be answered within 30 days, free of charge.

An unfamiliar name in the log is not necessarily a problem – a secretary scanning a letter or a doctor reviewing a referral will also appear. If something worries you, you can ask the hospital or clinic to explain, in writing if it concerns a hospital.

What if something is wrong?

Mistakes happen: a wrong allergy, an outdated medical history from another country, or something you said that was recorded inaccurately. You can ask for it to be corrected.

Correction

Under section 42 of the Health Personnel Act, corrections are made by a new entry or a dated and signed correction. The original text may not be made unreadable – so you will see both what was written and that it has been corrected.

Deletion

Deletion is stricter. The Directorate of Health explains that information can be deleted if, among other things, it is incorrect or misleading and also a burden to you, or not needed for your care. You should be told what deleting it could mean for the doctors who treat you later.

If your request is refused

A refusal and the reasons for it must be written in your record, and you must be told that you can appeal to the County Governor (Statsforvalteren), the state body that handles patient complaints.

Helping a parent or partner

If you help an older relative or a partner who does not read Norwegian well, they can give you power of attorney on Helsenorge. See our article Power of attorney on Helsenorge.

Good to know

Your record is a working tool for your doctors, but it is about you. Asking questions about it is completely normal in Norway – a conversation with your GP often solves more than a formal complaint.
